Emergency Contraception Guide

How the Pill Affects Your Body & Cycle

📅 Follow-Up: Take a pregnancy test on — if your period hasn't arrived or is more than 7 days late.

Calculated 21 days (3 weeks) from your logged pill timestamp.

  • 📆
    Cycle Shift: Your next period might arrive a few days early or up to a week late because emergency pills work primarily by delaying ovulation.
  • 🩹
    Short-Term Body Effects: Light spotting/bleeding, temporary fatigue, mild lower-belly cramping, breast tenderness, or mild nausea for a few days is completely normal.
  • ⏱️
    The 2-Hour Rule: If you vomit within 2 hours of taking the emergency pill, your body may not have absorbed it—speak to a pharmacist or campus clinic as you may need another dose.
  • 🧠
    How Hormonal Pills Affect Your Mind & Body: Hormonal contraceptives and emergency pills use synthetic progestin to pause ovulation and lower natural estrogen and free testosterone—which can temporarily shift your mood, stress resilience, energy, and attraction.
